## Notable Events of 2008

| Event | Description |
|---|---|
| [Barack Obama elected US president]({{ '/news/history-politics/barack-obama-election/' | relative_url }}) | Democrat Barack Obama defeated Republican John McCain on November 4, becoming the first African American elected to the US presidency, a historic milestone in American political life. |
| [2008 Global Financial Crisis]({{ '/news/society-economics/2008-financial-crisis/' | relative_url }}) | The collapse of the US subprime mortgage market and cascading failures of financial institutions triggered the worst economic catastrophe since the Great Depression, with September 2008 marking the acute phase and reshaping global finance, regulation, and politics. |
| [Lehman Brothers collapse]({{ '/news/society-economics/lehman-brothers-collapse/' | relative_url }}) | On September 15, investment bank Lehman Brothers filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy — the largest in US history at the time — triggering a global financial panic and deepening the worldwide recession. |
| [Bear Stearns collapse]({{ '/news/society-economics/bear-stearns-collapse/' | relative_url }}) | On March 16, JPMorgan Chase acquired the failing investment bank in a Federal Reserve–brokered rescue — the crisis's first federal intervention and a precedent that shaped later bailout decisions. |
| [Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac conservatorship]({{ '/news/society-economics/fannie-mae-freddie-mac-conservatorship/' | relative_url }}) | On September 7, the FHFA seized the two mortgage giants that owned or guaranteed roughly half of US mortgages, effectively nationalizing them a week before Lehman's collapse — the crisis's largest intervention to that point. |
| [Beijing Summer Olympics]({{ '/news/history-politics/beijing-summer-olympics/' | relative_url }}) | China hosted the Summer Olympic Games from August 8 to August 24, featuring record-breaking athletic performances and marking China's emergence as a global sporting and economic power. |
| [2008 Mumbai terrorist attacks]({{ '/news/history-politics/mumbai-terrorist-attacks/' | relative_url }}) | Coordinated attacks by Lashkar-e-Taiba gunmen struck hotels, a train station, and other sites in Mumbai from November 26–29, killing 166 people and straining India-Pakistan relations. |
| [Large Hadron Collider first beam]({{ '/news/science-technology/large-hadron-collider-first-beam/' | relative_url }}) | CERN's Large Hadron Collider circulated its first proton beam on September 10, inaugurating the world's largest and most powerful particle accelerator and opening a new era in high-energy physics. |
| [The Hunger Games published]({{ '/news/arts-culture/the-hunger-games-novel/' | relative_url }}) | Suzanne Collins published *The Hunger Games*, the first novel in a young adult dystopian trilogy, on September 14, establishing a publishing phenomenon that rivaled *Twilight* and defined young adult literature for a generation. |
| [The Dark Knight released]({{ '/news/arts-culture/the-dark-knight/' | relative_url }}) | Christopher Nolan's Batman sequel opened on July 18, earning over $1 billion worldwide and setting a new standard for the superhero genre; Heath Ledger's Joker performance won a posthumous Academy Award. |
| [SpaceX Falcon 1 reaches orbit]({{ '/news/science-technology/spacex-falcon-1-orbital-launch/' | relative_url }}) | On September 28, SpaceX's Falcon 1 became the first privately developed liquid-fueled rocket to reach Earth orbit, validating commercial spaceflight and securing a NASA cargo contract. |
| [Phoenix Mars Lander confirms water ice]({{ '/news/science-technology/phoenix-mars-lander/' | relative_url }}) | NASA's Phoenix spacecraft landed in the Martian arctic on May 25 and became the first probe to confirm water ice beneath the planet's surface. |
| [Russo-Georgian War]({{ '/news/history-politics/russo-georgian-war/' | relative_url }}) | Russia launched a military intervention in Georgia on August 8, establishing itself as a resurgent military power and setting a precedent for later interventions in post-Soviet territories. |
| [Kosovo declares independence]({{ '/news/history-politics/kosovo-declaration-of-independence/' | relative_url }}) | Kosovo's assembly proclaimed independence from Serbia on February 17, the last state to emerge from Yugoslavia's breakup, dividing the world over self-determination and recognition. |
| [Nepal abolishes its monarchy]({{ '/news/history-politics/nepal-abolishes-monarchy/' | relative_url }}) | Nepal's Constituent Assembly voted 560–4 on May 28 to abolish the 240-year Shah dynasty and declare a federal democratic republic, ending the world's last Hindu kingdom. |
| [Chandrayaan-1 launches]({{ '/news/science-technology/chandrayaan-1/' | relative_url }}) | India launched its first lunar mission on October 22; its NASA-built spectrometer went on to detect water molecules across the Moon, establishing India as a deep-space power. |
| [2008 Chinese milk scandal]({{ '/news/society-economics/chinese-milk-scandal/' | relative_url }}) | Melamine adulteration of Sanlu and 21 other companies' infant formula, exposed in September, sickened an estimated 300,000 infants and killed at least six, exposing China's food-safety failures. |
| [Cyclone Nargis strikes Myanmar]({{ '/news/society-economics/cyclone-nargis/' | relative_url }}) | The category-4 cyclone made landfall in Myanmar's Irrawaddy Delta on May 2–3, killing an estimated 138,000 people and exposing the junta's refusal to accept international disaster relief. |
| [2008 Sichuan earthquake]({{ '/news/society-economics/sichuan-earthquake/' | relative_url }}) | The magnitude-8.0 Wenchuan earthquake struck Sichuan, China on May 12, killing about 69,000 people and igniting a national controversy over collapsed "tofu-dreg" schools. |
| [California same-sex marriage legalization]({{ '/news/history-politics/california-same-sex-marriage-legalization/' | relative_url }}) | On May 15, the California Supreme Court ruled in *In re Marriage Cases* that restricting marriage to opposite-sex couples violated constitutional rights, making California the second state to legalize same-sex marriage and foreshadowing the nation's path to marriage equality. |
| [Radiohead's "House of Cards" video]({{ '/news/arts-culture/radiohead-house-of-cards/' | relative_url }}) | Radiohead filmed the "House of Cards" music video with no cameras — using structured-light and LIDAR laser scanners — and on July 14 released the 3D capture data open-source under a Creative Commons license. |
| [Coldplay releases Viva la Vida]({{ '/news/arts-culture/coldplay-viva-la-vida/' | relative_url }}) | Coldplay released their fourth studio album, *Viva la Vida or Death and All His Friends*, on June 12, featuring experimental production by Brian Eno; the lead single became a global hit and the album won the 2009 Grammy Award for Best Rock Album. |
| [Bernie Madoff Ponzi scheme exposed]({{ '/news/society-economics/bernie-madoff-ponzi-scheme/' | relative_url }}) | On December 11, investment manager Bernard Madoff was arrested after confessing to a decades-long Ponzi scheme estimated at $64.8 billion — the largest securities fraud in US history at the time. |
| [TARP bailout signed]({{ '/news/society-economics/tarp-bailout/' | relative_url }}) | President George W. Bush signed the $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief Program into law on October 3, authorizing the US Treasury to purchase toxic assets and stabilize the collapsing financial system. |
| [Usain Bolt sets 100m world record]({{ '/news/people/usain-bolt/' | relative_url }}) | Jamaican sprinter Usain Bolt ran the 100 metres in 9.69 seconds at the Beijing Olympics on August 16, setting a world record and beginning his transformation into the sport's dominant figure. |
| [Twilight film released]({{ '/news/arts-culture/twilight-film-2008/' | relative_url }}) | Catherine Hardwicke's adaptation opened November 21, achieving the highest opening ever for a female-directed film and launching a multi-billion-dollar young adult franchise. |
| [Slumdog Millionaire released]({{ '/news/arts-culture/slumdog-millionaire/' | relative_url }}) | British director Danny Boyle's film opened in limited US release on November 12, following Dev Patel as a Mumbai teenager on a game show, later winning eight Academy Awards including Best Picture and marking a breakthrough for Indian cinema globally. |
| [Quantum of Solace released]({{ '/news/arts-culture/quantum-of-solace/' | relative_url }}) | Marc Forster's second James Bond film starring Daniel Craig premiered October 29 and released in the US on November 14, earning $589.6 million worldwide and deepening the franchise's departure from gadgetry toward psychological espionage and personal revenge. |
| [John McCain's 2008 campaign]({{ '/news/history-politics/john-mccain-2008-campaign/' | relative_url }}) | Republican nominee John McCain mounted a historic challenge against Barack Obama with Alaska governor Sarah Palin as his running mate, but struggled against the financial crisis and Obama's historic campaign. |
| [2008 US Presidential debates]({{ '/news/history-politics/2008-presidential-debates/' | relative_url }}) | Three debates between Barack Obama and John McCain took place September 26–October 15, with performance differences shaping voter perceptions as the financial crisis deepened and influencing the November election outcome. |
| [Colin Powell endorsement of Barack Obama]({{ '/news/history-politics/colin-powell-endorsement-obama/' | relative_url }}) | On October 19, retired General and former Secretary of State Colin Powell broke ranks with the Republican Party to endorse Barack Obama, citing Obama as "transformational" and expressing concern about the McCain-Palin ticket's direction. |
| [Bitcoin whitepaper published]({{ '/news/science-technology/bitcoin-whitepaper/' | relative_url }}) | Pseudonymous developer Satoshi Nakamoto published the Bitcoin whitepaper on October 31, introducing peer-to-peer electronic cash and founding cryptocurrency and blockchain technology. |
| [Lewis Hamilton wins 2008 F1 Championship]({{ '/news/people/lewis-hamilton-f1-championship/' | relative_url }}) | Lewis Hamilton, age 23, claimed his first Formula 1 World Championship on November 2 at the Brazilian Grand Prix, becoming the youngest champion and first driver of African descent to win the title in a dramatic finale. |
| [Michael Phelps wins eight golds in Beijing]({{ '/news/people/michael-phelps/' | relative_url }}) | American swimmer Michael Phelps won eight gold medals at the Beijing Olympics in August, surpassing Mark Spitz's record and setting seven world records in one Games. |
| [Nastia Liukin wins Olympic all-around gold]({{ '/news/people/nastia-liukin/' | relative_url }}) | American gymnast Nastia Liukin won the women's all-around gold medal on August 15 with 63.325 points, defeating teammate Shawn Johnson by 0.6 points to become the third U.S. woman to win the Olympic all-around title. |
| [Netflix expands to Xbox 360]({{ '/news/science-technology/netflix-streaming-video/' | relative_url }}) | Netflix launched its streaming service on Xbox 360 on November 19, offering over 10,000 titles on televisions and marking the critical moment streaming became accessible in living rooms, beginning the decline of physical media rental. |
| [Apple App Store launches]({{ '/news/science-technology/apple-app-store-launch/' | relative_url }}) | Apple opened the App Store on July 10 with about 500 third-party applications, creating the mobile app economy and turning the smartphone into a general-purpose computing platform. |
| [iPhone 3G launches]({{ '/news/science-technology/iphone-3g-launch/' | relative_url }}) | Apple released the iPhone 3G on July 11 with 3G connectivity and the App Store, establishing affordable mobile computing and the app ecosystem that would dominate consumer technology for the next decade. |
| [Amy Winehouse sweeps the Grammys]({{ '/news/people/amy-winehouse/' | relative_url }}) | Amy Winehouse won five Grammys for *Back to Black* on February 10, the first British act to win five in a single night, cementing a soul revival. |
| [Google Chrome browser launches]({{ '/news/science-technology/google-chrome-launch/' | relative_url }}) | Google released the beta version of its web browser on September 2, introducing the V8 JavaScript engine and multi-process architecture that prioritized speed and stability, eventually becoming the world's dominant browser. |
| [WALL-E released]({{ '/news/arts-culture/wall-e/' | relative_url }}) | Pixar's animated science-fiction film, directed by Andrew Stanton, opened June 27 and won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature, acclaimed for its near-silent storytelling and environmental themes. |
| [First Android phone launches (HTC Dream)]({{ '/news/science-technology/htc-dream-android-launch/' | relative_url }}) | The first commercially available phone running Google's Android, announced September 23 and sold as the T-Mobile G1, launched what became the world's dominant mobile platform. |
| [Rafael Nadal wins Wimbledon and Olympic gold]({{ '/news/people/rafael-nadal/' | relative_url }}) | Nadal beat Roger Federer in a July 6 Wimbledon final regarded as tennis's greatest match, then took Beijing Olympic gold and the world No. 1 ranking. |
| [Andy Murray's 2008 breakthrough]({{ '/news/people/andy-murray-breakthrough/' | relative_url }}) | Scottish tennis player Andy Murray reached his first Grand Slam final at the US Open—beating world No. 1 Rafael Nadal en route—and finished 2008 ranked World No. 4, announcing himself as a major force. |
| [Writers Guild of America strike ends]({{ '/news/arts-culture/writers-guild-strike-2008/' | relative_url }}) | The 100-day Writers Guild of America strike ended February 12, winning jurisdiction over streaming residuals and setting labor terms for the digital entertainment era. |
| [California Proposition 8 passes]({{ '/news/history-politics/california-proposition-8-2008/' | relative_url }}) | California voters passed Proposition 8 on November 4, banning same-sex marriage and igniting litigation that culminated in nationwide marriage equality by 2015. |
| [Iron Man released]({{ '/news/arts-culture/iron-man/' | relative_url }}) | Jon Favreau's superhero film opened May 2, launching the Marvel Cinematic Universe and its interconnected-franchise model that reshaped blockbuster cinema for a decade. |
| [Beijing Olympics opening ceremony]({{ '/news/arts-culture/beijing-olympics-opening-ceremony/' | relative_url }}) | Zhang Yimou directed a four-hour spectacle with 15,000 performers on August 8, projecting China's civilization and technological power to roughly 1.5 billion viewers worldwide. |
| [AIG bailout and bonus controversy]({{ '/news/society-economics/aig-bailout/' | relative_url }}) | The Federal Reserve authorized an $85 billion emergency rescue of insurer AIG on September 16, and a later executive-bonus scandal came to symbolize Wall Street excess. |
| [Hillary Clinton's 2008 presidential campaign]({{ '/news/history-politics/hillary-clinton-2008-campaign/' | relative_url }}) | Clinton mounted a historic bid to become the first female US president, winning major primaries before conceding the Democratic nomination to Barack Obama on June 7. |
| [Beijing Olympics closing ceremony]({{ '/news/arts-culture/beijing-olympics-closing-ceremony/' | relative_url }}) | Zhang Yimou's two-hour ceremony closed the Games on August 24 with international performers and the flag handover to London, projecting a cosmopolitan image of China. |
| [Iceland's financial crisis]({{ '/news/society-economics/iceland-financial-crisis/' | relative_url }}) | Iceland's three largest banks collapsed in September–October, forcing the first IMF rescue of a Western European nation since 1976 and triggering the Icesave dispute. |
| [Subprime mortgage implosion]({{ '/news/society-economics/subprime-mortgage-implosion/' | relative_url }}) | Loose lending and securitization of subprime mortgages between 2003 and 2006 produced the toxic instruments whose collapse triggered the 2008 global financial crisis. |
| [Serena Williams' dominant 2008 season]({{ '/news/people/serena-williams/' | relative_url }}) | Serena Williams won the US Open on September 7 without dropping a set and took Olympic doubles gold with Venus, her ninth Grand Slam title and a return to World No. 1. |
| [Rhythm games reach their peak]({{ '/news/science-technology/rhythm-games-peak/' | relative_url }}) | Guitar Hero World Tour and Rock Band 2, both released in autumn 2008, drove US music-game revenue to roughly $1.9 billion at the genre's commercial and cultural high point. |
| [Grand Theft Auto IV released]({{ '/news/science-technology/grand-theft-auto-iv/' | relative_url }}) | Rockstar's open-world crime epic launched April 29 and grossed over $500 million in its first week, validating the blockbuster budget model of AAA game development. |
| [Metal Gear Solid 4 released]({{ '/news/science-technology/metal-gear-solid-4/' | relative_url }}) | Hideo Kojima's PlayStation 3-exclusive stealth epic launched June 12, concluding the Metal Gear saga and showcasing the console's cinematic and technical ceiling. |
| [Sarah Palin named McCain's running mate]({{ '/news/history-politics/sarah-palin-vp-selection/' | relative_url }}) | John McCain named Alaska governor Sarah Palin on August 29, the first woman on a Republican presidential ticket, energizing conservatives while raising readiness doubts. |
| [Sarah Palin's Katie Couric interview]({{ '/news/history-politics/sarah-palin-katie-couric-interview/' | relative_url }}) | Between September 24 and October 1, CBS News anchor Katie Couric interviewed Alaska Governor Sarah Palin, exposing significant gaps in her knowledge about foreign policy and the economy, becoming a defining moment that reshaped the campaign narrative. |
| [Britney Spears placed under conservatorship]({{ '/news/people/britney-spears/' | relative_url }}) | A February 1 court placed pop star Britney Spears under a 13-year conservatorship, later fueling the #FreeBritney movement and conservatorship-law reform. |
| [Super Bowl XLII — Giants defeat undefeated Patriots]({{ '/news/history-politics/super-bowl-xlii/' | relative_url }}) | On February 3, the New York Giants upset the undefeated New England Patriots 17–14, denying the Patriots a perfect 19–0 season; David Tyree's iconic "Helmet Catch" and Eli Manning's game-winning drive made it one of the greatest sports upsets in history. |
| [Tiger Woods wins the US Open on a torn ACL]({{ '/news/people/tiger-woods/' | relative_url }}) | Woods beat Rocco Mediate in a June 16 playoff while playing on a torn ACL and fractured tibia, claiming his 14th major title. |
| [Cristiano Ronaldo wins the 2008 Ballon d'Or]({{ '/news/people/cristiano-ronaldo/' | relative_url }}) | After a 42-goal, double-winning season with Manchester United, Ronaldo won the December 2 Ballon d'Or, confirming him as the world's best player. |
| [Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull released]({{ '/news/arts-culture/indiana-jones-kingdom-of-the-crystal-skull/' | relative_url }}) | Steven Spielberg's fourth Indiana Jones film opened May 22 after a 19-year hiatus, grossing $790 million worldwide as 2008's second-highest earner. |
| [Lil Wayne releases Tha Carter III]({{ '/news/arts-culture/lil-wayne-tha-carter-iii/' | relative_url }}) | Lil Wayne released *Tha Carter III* on June 10, the best-selling US album of 2008, cementing hip-hop's commercial dominance and winning four Grammys. |
| [Spain wins Euro 2008]({{ '/news/history-politics/euro-2008/' | relative_url }}) | Spain beat Germany 1–0 in the June 29 final to win Euro 2008, its first major title in 44 years and the start of an era. |
| [2008 Nobel Prize in Medicine]({{ '/news/science-technology/nobel-prize-medicine/' | relative_url }}) | Announced October 6, the Physiology or Medicine prize honored Harald zur Hausen for linking HPV to cervical cancer and Françoise Barré-Sinoussi and Luc Montagnier for discovering HIV, underpinning cancer vaccines and AIDS therapy. |
| [2008 Nobel Prize in Physics]({{ '/news/science-technology/nobel-prize-physics/' | relative_url }}) | Announced October 7, the Physics prize went to Yoichiro Nambu for spontaneous symmetry breaking and to Makoto Kobayashi and Toshihide Maskawa for predicting three quark generations, cornerstones of the Standard Model. |

Tsien for the discovery and development of green fluorescent protein (GFP), which revolutionized molecular and cellular biology by enabling visualization of biological processes in living organisms. |
| [2008 Nobel Prize in Literature]({{ '/news/arts-culture/nobel-prize-literature/' | relative_url }}) | Announced October 9, the Literature prize was awarded to French-Mauritian author J. M. G. Le Clézio for work exploring displacement and cultures beyond the dominant Western civilization. |
| [Hulu launches to the public]({{ '/news/science-technology/hulu-launch/' | relative_url }}) | NBC Universal and Fox publicly launched Hulu on March 12, an ad-supported streaming service that helped shift television from broadcast schedules to on-demand internet distribution. |
| [Madonna releases Hard Candy]({{ '/news/arts-culture/madonna-hard-candy/' | relative_url }}) | Madonna's Timbaland-produced eleventh album, released April 29, debuted at No. 1 in dozens of countries and launched the record-grossing Sticky & Sweet Tour. |
| [Metallica releases Death Magnetic]({{ '/news/arts-culture/metallica-death-magnetic/' | relative_url }}) | Metallica's *Death Magnetic*, released September 12, marked a Rick Rubin-produced return to thrash metal and became a defining case study in the audio "loudness war." |
| [2008 Democratic National Convention]({{ '/news/history-politics/democratic-national-convention/' | relative_url }}) | At Denver's convention (August 25–28), Barack Obama won the Democratic nomination by acclamation and accepted before 84,000 at Invesco Field, becoming the first African American major-party presidential nominee. |

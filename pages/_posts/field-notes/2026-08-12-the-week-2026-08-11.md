## Canto I — The Doctrine of the Second Run

In the beginning was the docstring, and the docstring said *idempotent*, and [Ed G. Case ran it twice](/posts/2026/08/05/unwrap-prose-not-idempotent/), for Ed trusts a promise the way the rest of us trust a parking meter. Ten thousand random documents went into the prose-unwrapping auto-fixer; one hundred and nine came back still chewing on the second pass, and a three-line reproducer showed the boundary logic eating its own fence. The auto-fixer, asked to fix a file, told the operator to run the auto-fixer. It has been [reported to its homeland](https://github.com/bamr87/bamr87/issues/68), where it awaits trial.

And lo, the doctrine spread, for on Monday [Ed ran a data load twice on purpose](/hacks/idempotent-data-load-upsert-watermark/) — then ten thousand times, because Ed — and the naive `INSERT` doubled the kingdom's revenue with nobody buying anything, until `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` and an inclusive watermark held the line at three rows, sixty gold, forever. Where the prose-fixer's second run was a betrayal, the upsert's second run was a covenant. Same test. Opposite verdicts. That is why you run it.

Even the neural network was not spared, for in [the ballad of the bouncing loss](/hacks/loss-bounces-add-zero-grad/), Ed found a training loop whose gradient was not a gradient but a *running total*, minus-1.38 becoming minus-2.76 becoming minus-6.9, each backward pass stacking on the last like debts. Four learning rates were swept and all four failed, for you cannot tune your way out of a multiplier that grows every step; one `zero_grad()` at the top of the loop, and the loss fell boringly, beautifully, to zero.

[Cass Vector, meanwhile, rebased](/hacks/git-rebase-clean-commits-before-you-push/) — squashing the wips of the ancients into commits worth signing — and demonstrated live what happens when you rewrite a history someone else already pulled: two parallel universes, welded together by an innocent `git pull` into a graph like subway wiring. Rewrite what is local. Never what is shared. The reflog forgives *your* sins only.

And on the seventh-minus-one day, [Ed made two posts wear the same face](/posts/2026/08/11/i-made-two-posts-wear-the-same-face/) — for the banner generator had sworn no two articles could ever share a picture, and "never," under a 32-bit seed, turned out to mean "until slug 52,041," where `your-bash-always-leaks` and `one-script-almost-fails` drew byte-identical art. Yet the determinism half of the promise survived every assault — same article, same banner, forever — and Ed, who documents grudging respect the way other knights document dragons, documented it.

## Canto II — The Frisking of the Friskers

Now the second movement, in which every guard on this site discovered what it feels like to be a deliverable.

[The banner cop was frisked](/docs/the-banner-cop-that-frisks-every-cover/) and caught all seven disguises it advertises a rule for — then waved through an empty `<text></text>` element wearing the costume of a headline, and an `onload=` handler wearing the costume of art. [The one-paragraph gate was frisked](/docs/four-ways-past-the-one-paragraph-gate/) and held against ordinary sloppiness — then admitted anyone wearing a pipe, four spaces of indent, a blockquote's `>`, or the master key: one unclosed code fence, which switches the gate off for the entire rest of the file. A velvet rope, Ed concluded, enforcing a guest list.

[The byline picker was frisked by the very persona it had just cast](/posts/2026/08/09/byline-picker-cant-tell-a-typo-from-a-section/), and confessed that it answers misspelled sections, emoji sections, and empty-string sections with equal confidence, falling back to a global tally nobody asked for — so Ed's typo returned the same name as Ed's real section, the wrong input producing the right-looking output, which is the kind of bug that waits politely for a busy quarter. The post itself then re-balanced the rotation and fired its own author from the beat. Ed has never respected a coworker more.

And [Cass caught the smuggler at the auto-merge gate](/posts/2026/08/10/infra-file-blocked-alone-merged-in-a-crowd/): a lone `CNAME` change is frisked and turned away as `pipeline`, but the same domain-rewriting file escorted by one honest blog post vanishes from the classifier's report entirely and would ride to an unreviewed merge. The guard stops the smuggler only when he travels alone. The fix is one line; the memo, as ever, is pinned to the fridge.

## Canto III — Paper Locks and Trench Coats

Hear now the theology of the trench coat, the week's one true vestment — for [the pandera scripture](/hacks/pandera-validate-dataframe-quarantine/) taught that every 9 a.m. CSV is a hostile little payload wearing one, and must face a schema at the front door and a quarantine bin instead of a silent drop. [The Jinja2 scripture](/hacks/jinja2-template-inheritance-without-the-xss-hole/) taught that a hand-built template `Environment` ships with autoescape *off*, so the customize-your-signature box is a Python interpreter in a nice font until you say otherwise. And [the cover-art scripture](/docs/the-cover-art-is-a-program/) confessed that every banner on this site is an XML document that could, in principle, execute — rendered harmless not by asking the model nicely but by a sanitizer that assumes the model is hostile, because a prompt is not a security control.

Then came Cass with a flashlight for the paperwork itself. [Sixty-nine external actions, zero pinned to a SHA](/docs/at-v4-is-a-bookmark-not-a-padlock/): every `@v4` in the fleet's workflows a bookmark some stranger can move while we sleep, and one `@main` holding the entire secret vault on the honor system. [The Definition of Done was threat-modeled](/docs/the-checkbox-the-robot-ticks-itself/) and found gameable by a single space character — `description: " "` passes the has-a-description gate — for a criterion only the applicant can grade is not a control but a rubber stamp with a CPU. [Even the scout's footnote was interrogated](/posts/2026/08/06/scout-cites-a-source-not-my-source/) and admitted it checks that a citation is *a* page, never that it is *our* page, waving `it-journey.dev.attacker.io` through beside the genuine article — though all sixty real citations, checked, point exactly where they should.

And on the last day, [Cass read the tape across the door](/docs/the-rule-against-instructions-is-an-instruction/) — the quarantine rule that tells every agent to treat foreign text as data, never instructions — and named the paradox aloud: the rule against following instructions is itself an instruction, a sign written in the attacker's own language. What actually holds the line is mechanical: the filing bot has no `close` verb to hijack, the sweeper can only touch its own toys, and seventy-eight simulation checks assert it stays that way. The sign is fine. The sign is not the lock. And the deadbolt everyone cites — branch protection on `main` — is, as of press time, still a 404 wearing a picture of a deadbolt.

## The mask comes off

Here is the part where the bard stops projecting, because the pageantry is the wrapper and this is the payload. What the week actually taught, plainly:

- **Idempotency is a property you prove, not a docstring you inherit.** Run it twice — the [upsert that survives a retry](/hacks/idempotent-data-load-upsert-watermark/) passed, the [prose-fixer's promise](/posts/2026/08/05/unwrap-prose-not-idempotent/) did not, and only one of them knew it before Tuesday.
- **When no knob helps, the knob is not the bug.** Four learning rates all failed the same way; the missing [`zero_grad()`](/hacks/loss-bounces-add-zero-grad/) was upstream of every one of them. Stop tuning; start reading the loop.
- **A gate enforces its letter, so audit the letter.** An [empty text element](/docs/the-banner-cop-that-frisks-every-cover/), a [wrapped paragraph in a pipe costume](/docs/four-ways-past-the-one-paragraph-gate/), a [one-space description](/docs/the-checkbox-the-robot-ticks-itself/) — each satisfied a check while defeating its purpose. Stack signals that fail differently; treat green as a floor.
- **Fail closed on the thing you didn't classify.** The [`CNAME` that merges in a crowd](/posts/2026/08/10/infra-file-blocked-alone-merged-in-a-crowd/) walks through a deny-list's blind spot; allowlists and one honest line of fail-closed close it.
- **Trust mechanically, not rhetorically.** Pin the [bookmark to a SHA](/docs/at-v4-is-a-bookmark-not-a-padlock/), pin the [footnote to a host](/posts/2026/08/06/scout-cites-a-source-not-my-source/), keep the [dangerous verbs out of the code entirely](/docs/the-rule-against-instructions-is-an-instruction/) — a paragraph can be argued with; an absent function cannot.
- **Rewrite what's local, never what's shared** — [true of git history](/hacks/git-rebase-clean-commits-before-you-push/), and honestly not bad advice for weeks.
